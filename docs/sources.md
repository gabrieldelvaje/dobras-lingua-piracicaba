## Scientific references

### Sturtevant, A. H. (1940)

**A New Inherited Character in Man.** Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, 26(2), 100-102.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.26.2.100

PubMed / PMC: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1078012/

This historical paper is the origin of the widely repeated hereditary interpretation of tongue rolling. The present project does not treat its simple inheritance model as established modern consensus.

### Matlock, P. (1952)

**Identical twins discordant in tongue-rolling.** Journal of Heredity, 43(1), 24.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1093/oxfordjournals.jhered.a106251

Oxford Academic: https://academic.oup.com/jhered/article-abstract/43/1/24/903604

Matlock reported discordance for tongue rolling among monozygotic twin pairs, evidence against a strictly deterministic single-gene explanation.

### Martin, N. G. (1975)

**No evidence for a genetic basis of tongue rolling or hand clasping.** Journal of Heredity, 66(3), 179-180.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1093/oxfordjournals.jhered.a108608

PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1236879/

### Ebeye, O. A. (2019)

**A study on tongue rolling, tongue folding and cerumen type in a Nigeria population.** Anatomy Journal of Africa, 8(2), 1540-1543.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.4314/aja.v8i2.188220

Article: https://www.ajol.info/index.php/aja/article/view/188220

The study examined 400 adult Esan participants in southern Nigeria and reported 72.9% tongue rollers.

### Adekoya, K. O., Fakorede, S. T., Ogunkanmi, A. L., Amusa, O. D., Sifau, M. O., & Oboh, B. (2020)

**Inheritance pattern and association studies of some human morphogenetic traits among Nigerian undergraduate students.** Scientific African, 9, e00508.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.sciaf.2020.e00508

The study assessed 1,000 undergraduate students at the University of Lagos and reported 63.9% tongue rollers.

## Use of the literature in this repository

The literature values are contextual comparisons only. They are not combined into a meta-analysis, and differences between studies should not be attributed solely to geography or genetics because sampling frames, ages, methods and populations differ.
